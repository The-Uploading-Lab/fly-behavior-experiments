The Fly Hall of Fame

Every fly that has held the title, what it was judged on, and what it could not do. These are the flies working out whether a measured connectome can be turned into a mind that behaves.

A champion here is not a different animal. It is the same measured fly wiring, tuned a different way, that stayed on its feet more often than the fly before it. The wiring is not ours and we do not touch it: it was traced from a real Drosophila, synapse by synapse, by other labs. What we search for is the rest of the animal, the numbers the map does not contain, like how fast a leg joint springs back or how hard a foot has to be pressed before it lets go. Find a set that makes the wiring walk, and you have found a candidate explanation for how the real fly does it.

How a title is won here. The test is written down before the fly is run, on random starting conditions nobody has seen, and the bar is never widened to let a fly through. A challenger has to beat the holder outright, not tie it. When the test gets harder, the old test closes rather than reopening, so a past champion is never stripped: it keeps exactly what it won, and each card tells you what its test measured, which is also a list of what nobody asked it.
The three flies

Three real animals sit under this whole project, and every result belongs to one of them. They are not interchangeable. Two are male, one is female, and one of the males is missing his brain, which is exactly the kind of thing that shows up in the numbers.

BANC the female

188,508 neurons, whole central nervous system

She is the only one of the three who is complete: brain and nerve cord together, one female fly mapped end to end. Every champion this project has ever crowned has been her. When a card below says a fly stood eight times in twelve, it means her wiring, tuned a particular way, stayed upright in eight runs out of twelve.

MANC male number one

102,369 neurons, nerve cord only, no brain

A male, and only the walking half of him. An insect does most of its walking in the ventral nerve cord, the rope of neurons running down its underside, and that is all of him that was ever mapped. He cannot be asked a question that begins in the brain, because his brain does not exist in our data. He has never held a title. His legs step, his load reflexes fire, and his left-right leg coordination is measurably better than hers. He just falls over.

MCNS male number two

211,577 neurons, whole central nervous system

A second male, and a far more complete one: brain included, and nearly every neuron carries a label where the first male's are blank. He joined the project because Robin asked a question nobody had answered, why this male and not that one, and the honest answer turned out to be that no reason had ever been recorded. He is the newest of the three and has not yet produced a title result.

Having three matters more than it sounds. With two animals, a disagreement is a coin toss and you cannot tell which one is odd. With three you can start asking whether a difference is about being male or just about being that particular fly. Sex is still tangled up with individual here, two males and one female, and that limit is recorded rather than waved away.
