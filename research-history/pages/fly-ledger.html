fly-wbe · the project from first principles

Can a wiring diagram become an animal?

A connectome tells us which neurons touch. It does not tell us how the animal runs. We take the fly's measured wiring, place it in a physical body, close the loop from sensors to neurons to muscles to the world, and ask one hard question: does it behave like a living fly?

The method

The goal is a lo-fi whole-brain emulation whose behaviour cannot be distinguished from a real fly on fixed measurements. We keep the anatomy measured and make every uncertainty visible.

01 · Measured wiring

Start with the connectome

We use published connectomes rather than drawing a circuit that produces the answer we want. We tune shared physiology, such as gains and time constants, but never add, remove, or individually reweight synapses.

02 · Closed loop

Give the network a body and a world

A brain is not its activity trace. The model has to sense, move, touch the ground, and receive the consequences of its own actions. Behaviour emerges from that full loop.

03 · Honest unknowns

Label guesses, then search them

Missing physiology becomes an explicit parameter, not a hidden zero. Measured values are constraints. Educated guesses come next. Unmeasured values are searched and remain labelled as guesses.

04 · Living benchmark

Grade the model against the animal

Every claim must survive measured neural or behavioural data. A conflict is useful because it eliminates a wrong model. Once a constraint is earned, it stays in the test battery.

Where the fly stands today

The walking question has moved from “can it stand?” to “can it go somewhere?”

r23 is the first champion required to step at real-fly cadence

It met the v3.1 title rule on four of five independent 12-seed batteries at measured input regularity. The failed battery is published beside the passes.

The open conflict: r23 walks at fly cadence and holds its body heading, but its movement has no preferred direction. Net speed is only 0.457 mm/s. The current model contains about 0.4% of the heading-integration circuitry in the simulated slice, while vision is the largest missing input to the descending layer. Lanes C and D now attack those two missing pieces directly.

The rules that keep the result honest

1

Register before running. Predictions, measures, and pass criteria are committed before the result exists.

2

Never grade on practice runs. Title matches use fresh seed blocks, followed by independent confirmation.

3

Keep failures and withdrawals. Broken instruments and wrong claims stay visible with the correction that replaced them.

4

One fly, not separate demos. As vision, navigation, smell, and flight are added, the same parameter set must keep every behaviour already earned.
