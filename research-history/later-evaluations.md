# Later evaluation supplement

Prepared 3 November 2026. This supplement records the subsequent review of the development history. The dated diary remains unchanged so that later corrections do not appear to have been known during development.

The August muscle-interface comparison contained 30 paired seeds. Its candidate stood in 16 trials and its predecessor in eight. Eleven pairs favoured the candidate and three favoured the predecessor, giving an exact two-sided discordance probability of 0.05737. Command identity changed from DNg100 to MDN, along with command rate, feedback and several dynamical parameters. This is evidence about the configuration package; an isolated stiffness benefit still needs a matched test. Antagonist coactivation and stiffness control have prior art, while the particular two-channel mapping and body interface are local implementation contributions.

An independent reconstruction of the September delay experiment produced median simulated speeds of 0.938 mm/s for the original assignment, 0.395 mm/s for uniform delays at the original mean and 0.346 mm/s for one shuffled assignment. All six original-versus-uniform and original-versus-shuffled seed comparisons favoured the original assignment. Twenty-eight of 36 trials matched five archived outcomes exactly. Eight had discrepancies, including one changed standing classification. The same shuffled vector was reused across six noise seeds. A test of anatomical specificity needs multiple shuffled assignments, matched mean and variance controls, and equal-budget refitting of all competing families. Whole-cell cable length remains a delay proxy.

The October inhibitory-strength comparison retained standing under several replacements for row-specific normalization. The exact-type median control stood in 24/24 trials with a conditional median speed of 2.4809 mm/s; the retained reference stood in 22/24 with 2.1647 mm/s. These source summaries were extracted without rerunning the simulations. The exact-type control was excluded from retention by the study design, so non-retention does not mean worse performance. Summed inhibitory weights are distinct from instantaneous inhibitory currents.

Walking and calcium checks exposed additional mismatches. Although the reconstructed animal stance reader agreed with supplied labels in 99.669–99.865% of leg frames, no replay supplied the required half-second within a reference speed bin. The H4 visual model's L1/L2 onset peaks occurred at 1.4 s, compared with animal medians of 0.45/0.40 s. Its normalized ten-second response errors were 0.249/0.696. These are feature errors from exposed data in a different model version. They do not establish independent neural validation of the walker.

The manuscript structure follows the use of model definitions, ablations and neural comparisons in [FlyVis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3), and the body and software documentation in [NeuroMechFly v2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02497-y). A related [October 2026 BANC embodiment preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33881) further narrows any claim about first connectome embodiment or delayed transmission. The candidate methods contribution concerns the motor interface, delay assignments, inhibitory scaling and their joint evaluation.

The [publication reproduction package](../publication/README.md) supplies the numeric records and executable analyses supporting these summaries. Remaining confirmatory work includes controlled muscle comparisons, delay-by-inhibition interactions, numerical convergence and ensembles evaluated on independent neural or perturbation measurements. These are proposed experiments.
