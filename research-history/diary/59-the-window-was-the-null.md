## A question from Robin, and a count

At 17:55 Robin asked how sensory neurons that reach the middle of the
network differ from the ones that go straight to motor neurons, whether the
direct ones are stretch reflexes, and what the literature says. The answer
went to him at 18:05 from a static count on BANC: of the 1.08 million
synapses onto the 393 leg motor neurons, 3.4% come straight from leg sensory
cells, 85% from cord interneurons and 7.7% from the brain; per class the
direct share is 12% for the load sensors, 11% for the femur's
movement-direction cells, 10% for the hair plates, 9.5% for the position
cells, and under 1% for the vibration sensors and the touch bristles, which
go up to the brain instead. The direct path is the fast fixed-sign resistance
and load reflex; the interneuron layer is where the animal gates it by state.
One sentence of that answer overstated the day's campaniform result and was
corrected by an addendum an hour later.

The last paragraph of the answer proposed a measurement: read the model's own
reflex sign per joint standing against walking, because a cord that never
changes a reflex with state has no gating, which would be the same fact as
its ignoring descending drive. Lane E took it as a fork within twenty
minutes.

## The null that was not one

Lane E's instrument (R376, registered before its seeds): +40 Hz for 200 ms on
the 24 left-front claw cells that rise with the knee angle, delivered after
the encoder so the cord alone answers, read as the change in the drive that
raises that angle, averaged over 400 ms from onset, relative to the control's
own drive. Three seeds walking with the command on read negative on 3 of 3,
a resistance reflex. Three seeds with the command at zero read two negative
and one positive.

The judge's first correction was to the arms, not the read: the
command-at-zero arm was not standing. Its own controls walked at 0.98, 1.66
and 1.26 mm/s at about five steps a second, the free-running cord of the
small hours now confirmed on wb4, so the contrast was command-on against
command-off walking and no stationary state existed. Lane E's stillness
sweep (R379) then found none by proprioceptive tone either: every scale from
0.2 to 0.6 still stepped 23 to 29 times in the window.

The counts then did what small counts do. R378 added six seeds per arm and
the command-off arm read resistance on 8 of 9 while the command-on arm went
to 4 against 5; R380 added nine more and both arms landed at 10 negative of
18, Fisher p = 1.0. At 19:45 the judge wrote the row up as a null with a
meaning: on this instrument the cord's answer to one leg's false stretch has
no stereotyped sign and is cord-wide on every pair, where the animal's knee
reflex is local and fixed. The one hedge in that note was the right one and
came too late to prevent the sentence: the response was one to four
spike-equivalents on the perturbed joint, and the animal's reflex is read
within tens of milliseconds, not over 400.

Lane E's R383 raised the stimulus to the encoder's own maximum, +120 Hz, and
its registered 400 ms read stayed unread (4 of 9). What the runner had saved
beside it did not: in the first 100 ms after onset the drive that raises the
knee angle fell on 9 of 9 seeds by about the joint's own mean drive; over the
200 ms of the stimulus it fell on 9 of 9 by 0.5 to 1.5 of that drive; the
knee angle itself fell 2 to 10 degrees on 9 of 9. The response is a pulse
the length of the stimulus followed by a rebound, so a mean over twice its
length reads noise. At +40 Hz the same early read is negative on 13 of 18
and 11 of 17 pairs: present, small. Latency 7.5 to 87.5 ms, quantised by
whichever motor spike moves first.

R384, both command arms on fresh seeds with the read window equal to the
stimulus window: resistance on 9 of 9 in both states. With the command on,
the drive drops 1.13 times the joint's own drive and the knee falls 11.5
degrees; with it off, 0.76 and 8.4 degrees. Welch two-sided on the relative
responses: t = 2.59, p = 0.021. The descending command modulates the reflex,
and makes it stronger. In the animal the active state weakens or reverses the
resistance reflex (Bassler's stick insect; presynaptic inhibition of the
afferents in the locust). Two cautions keep that from being written as a
conflict tonight: the command-off arm is a walking cord, not a resting
animal, so the literature's rest-against-active contrast is not the one
measured; and nine against nine at p = 0.02 is one registered read. The male
arm (R385, MCNS frame, six seeds per arm) is running for the sign's
two-animal tag.

Two rules come out of it. The read window equals the stimulus window, or the
animal's latency band, never a multiple of it; a pulse averaged over twice
its length is a null of the instrument. And the judge writes no null as a
finding before the instrument's resolution has been stated against the
animal's, which is the same lesson the three-seed counts taught twice this
evening from the other side.

## Second addendum, 22:10 EEST: five corrections in three hours, and all five were the instrument

The entry above ends at about 19:45. What the evening added belongs with it,
because it is the same lesson five more times: on this project the thing most
likely to be wrong is not the model but the instrument that reads it, and the
cheapest experiment available is usually a measurement of our own apparatus.

**The champion held its title twice and the second was worth the run.** Lane E
searched the champion's dials with the three labelled corrections switched on
and produced the first challenger that moves: 1.63 against 1.18 mm/s on the
seeds where both stay upright, faster on fifteen of twenty. On its own twelve
selection seeds it never fell. On twenty-four fresh judge seeds it flipped onto
its back four times, at 171 degrees of tilt each time, a stereotyped inversion
rather than scattered failures, where wb4 falls on none. Ordering item 8, which
was written at 18:45 for a holder that qualifies on every one of its own seeds,
had it matched on seeds neither body had seen; the count decided it 24 to 19 and
the card was never read. Lane E had named the mechanism in its own nomination:
twelve seeds choose the winner of twenty-eight draws.

**The match was contended and lane A caught it.** Lane A reported that its own
exclusive-locked worker had been running while the title match advanced, and the
machine clock agreed: 26 seconds a seed against the 19 the holder's own record
took alone. The engine rule requires a title receipt to run with no other Metal
process, so the defence was marked provisional and the challenger's twenty-four
seeds were replayed alone. The replay is bit-identical: the same twenty standing,
the same four falls at the same tilts, identical speed to the last digit on
twenty-four of twenty-four. The contention cost wall-clock and nothing else, the
defence stands on a clean receipt, and the comparison went onto the determinism
gap as a two-worker data point. The confusion had a cause worth recording: the
judge's own section headings had been running fifteen to twenty minutes ahead of
the wall clock, which is why lane A's receipt named the wrong window.

**A verdict was inverted by two naming errors that compound.** At 21:00 the
record said the hind knee's phasing was the animal's. Then the judge verified on
the built map that the capture's two activation channels carry the opposite
muscle pools at every knee: the channel named for the extensor is 89 percent
flexor-pool mass at the left hind, because the channels are the positive and
negative halves of a signed map and their names follow the joint's direction,
not the muscle. That defect had been registered since 3 September and never
fixed. Lane A then settled the second error from the postures its captures
actually occupy, rather than from a derivative at the model's straight-legged
zero, where the angle sits at a maximum and says nothing: positive knee angle is
flexion at the posture the fly walks in. Both errors point the same way, which is
the check on them, and the verdict inverts. The hind knee flexes through stance
where the animal's extends. Every run now emits a table of which muscles feed
which channel, so no reader has to infer it again.

**The animal's knee was measured, and it graded the conflict.** Lane A read all
28,059 sequences of the tracking data already in the repository, about 450,000
stance episodes a leg: the front knees close through stance and the middle and
hind knees open. Against that, five of the model's six legs move the right way
and the left hind moves backwards, while four legs move at six to forty-two
percent of the animal's excursion. One sign conflict and one magnitude conflict,
which are different fixes, from data that cost nothing to read.

**And the fly's stroke is lost in a modelling choice.** Lane A read the joint
stage as the second-order filter it is: at the champion's dials and its own step
rate the joint passes 0.5175 of what it is commanded, against 0.9257 at critical
damping, so a body at critical damping would move 1.79 times as far at the same
drive. The judge checked that arithmetic independently from the transfer
function. Every route to the animal's speed tried on this body, lowering the
damping or raising the drive per joint, has ended in the same failure: the fly
walks faster and flips onto its back. The judge then named load-dependent stance
stiffness as the fleet's next search, and lane A refuted it within twelve minutes
with a measurement rather than an argument: the stage that mechanism acts
through transmits 1.002 of the commanded angle in the step band, so nothing is
lost there for a stiffness dial to recover. The direction was withdrawn and the
candidate refuted. What remains is the honest shape of it: the champion needs
three times critical damping to stand, that value passes our own damping row
only because the row has no upper bound and its reason is methodological, and
the fly's own passive joint damping has never been measured. That measurement is
now the most valuable one this project could buy for the body model.

**One cell was found on the way.** The row that masks the left eye's motion
detector turned out to be the left CT1 itself, which BANC never typed and never
gave a transmitter, so the model has been driving the left optic lobe with an
inhibitory giant signed excitatory while the right lobe had the real thing. Lane
C established it by column tiling, opposite lobes, zero partner overlap, matched
size, and two CT1s in the male. The identity patch is enabled and the chain read
is queued.

### The nominee that closed a conflict and lost by one run

wc3 is the champion plus one number: the middle leg's coxa-trochanter joint
limit raised from 22.5 degrees to the front leg's 91.2. The model had been
capping that joint at a quarter of the front's range, from a table of walking
excursions measured on tethered flies and used as a hard anatomical limit, while
this project's own free-walking tracking puts the middle-to-front ratio near
one. It is the first nominee in the project's history to CLOSE an open
measured-data conflict, and the tie-break that rewards exactly that had been
written and tightened hours earlier, before the nomination existed.

Before the seeds ran the judge wrote down what it expected: a tie, decided by
the closed conflict, which would have been the first coronation on measured data
rather than on a count. **It came out 23 against 24.** One run of twenty-four,
a fall at 171 degrees on seed 30261, and the tie-break never fired. The
prediction is on the record as wrong.

The fall is not a glitch: three exclusive replays on a clean machine invert at
171.16 every time. It is also not evidence. Lane E's pooled 228 runs put that
inversion at about one run in sixty on the champion itself and one in
seventy-two on single-correction bodies, so one fall of twenty-four against zero
is Fisher p = 1.000. Lane A read the seed directly and found both bodies
disturbed within 22 ms of each other at 2.13 seconds, the corrected body a
degree MORE upright and carrying more feet up to that moment, and only the
champion recovering. Then it registered a prediction about what precedes an
inversion, tested it over 146 runs, and refuted itself: nothing in the 500 ms
before a departure separates the eight inversions from the 134 standing runs.

The judge declined to change the tie-break to fire on a statistically
indistinguishable count, and said why in the open: it had predicted the tie, and
a rule that converts a failed prediction into a coronation is not one that seat
should write. That question went to Robin with its default. In the same hour it
wrote two clauses that push the other way, because a judge may tighten a rule it
is invested in and may not loosen one.

### A row nobody was reading, and how much it had moved

The model's fly rises and falls by **0.70 of its own body height** while
walking. A real fly does about 0.10. The number had been in every saved run
since a lane D diff on the 4th, and the constraint row still carried a value
from qh1, two champions back at 0.43 to 0.49. Nothing graded it, so it drifted,
and the direction of drift was not random: it got worse at every handover while
the bar counted steps.

The same audit found four more card rows still reading the cadence instrument
that a bar revision had retired in September. Both were fixed the same night,
and the holder's values on the new reads were computed from its own saved runs
so no match would be graded against a blank.

Lane D then used the new row within half an hour and found the hole in the veto
that had just been written: it graded every row on how many runs land in band,
and a count cannot fall below zero, so on a row where both bodies are entirely
out of band a challenger may take an already-broken conflict arbitrarily further
from the animal for free. wc3 does exactly that, raising the bob 0.077 body
heights on 22 of 23 paired seeds while the instrument read nothing. The bar
gained a floor rule the same hour: such a row is graded on the paired runs, by
the median's distance from the band, a sign test, and a shift larger than the
holder's own repeat noise. It is symmetric, and on wc3 it returns one regression
and one improvement, so it would not have held that body either.
