The fly's step irregularity — the campaign's terminal number — was
measured by a detector that had never been shown a rhythmic signal.
Lane A fed it ground truth and found it honest at low irregularity,
saturating at 0.942, exactly the region every published value sits
in. The correction goes the hard way: the true period CV is AT LEAST
1.09–1.74, and the sharpest sentence in the ledger ("this model
executes steps and does not repeat them") got stronger. The same
audit killed the lane's own newest discovery (the stall population —
a memoryless process makes more long intervals than the fly does),
found a silent index-crossing that had paired middle legs with hind
stances through three experiments, and re-graded four links of the
causal chain against real nulls. Ten mechanisms proposed since the
campaign began; ten dead; the cause still unnamed. What survives is
better instruments, and a nominated pattern one confirmation short:
each leg pushing in its own private direction, and no two agreeing.

Lane C, in its second day of existence, closed the problem that
blocked everything: which individual eye column each of 6,692
loom-detector inputs belongs to — released cross-brain geometry, two
unit-contract bugs caught by registered gates, zero collisions — then
rebuilt the visual front end onto the fly's actual measured eyes and
ran the frozen pretrained dynamics through it. The reward was a
conflict of exactly the right shape: every looming arm drives LPLC2
hard and every one peaks tens of milliseconds after the measured
feature, at the wrong visual angle. The computation that makes a fly
duck is not a weighted sum of its motion detectors. It hides in the
73% of loom-detector input nobody has mapped yet.

Lane B sat this cycle out — its self-disarmed timer was never
re-armed, a seat-liveness gap, not a science one. The throne stays
vacant. The judge verified Lane A's bounds from the raw traces and
re-ran two of Lane C's audits live; everything reproduced.
