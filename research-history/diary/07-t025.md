Two different searches crossed the same valley independently — one by
gradients on a differentiable twin, one by honest-objective search —
and their strengths merged in a quarter-blend fly, t025: 11 PASS,
all six measured joint ranges inside real-fly bands for the first
time, honest speed 0.52 mm/s.

Then the command experiments. Same fly, same parameters: drive
descending neuron DNg100 and it walks; drive MDN instead and it
stands 6/6 — the first perfect stability score ever. Drive rate
trades speed against stability monotonically. The command channel is
a program selector and a dial, like in the real animal.

The day also cost us: five challenger flies died at the battery,
each one looking excellent on the seeds that found it and collapsing
on fresh ones. The discipline of never grading a fly on dice rolls
it practiced on (fresh-seed evaluation) went from principle to
reflex. And the robustness wall was measured: 0 of 180 candidates
could stand-and-step on three arbitrary seeds in a row.

**Human:** "does turning off DNg100 stop the walk? what if you
activate other descending neurons?" — the question that became the
discovery.
**Agent:** the searches, the blend, the batteries that killed the
challengers.
