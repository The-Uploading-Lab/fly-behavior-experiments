Two artifacts had been flattering every result for days. The
simulation tick was coarse enough to inflate step tempo; and foot
adhesion was permanently on, so "walking" flies were dragging
themselves with glued feet, and the load signal chattered fake steps.

Both fell to human eyes on videos. Robin: "the latest video barely
walks... what is it that is difficult?" — which forced the honest
lift measure (a step counts only if the foot's tip actually leaves
the ground). And an earlier "it tipped over" catch, when a video
contradicted the numbers we'd reported, created the parity rule:
every video's displacement and uprightness must match its simulation
record to the digit before it ships. That rule caught a renderer bug
as recently as today.

With honest measures and releasable feet, the first genuinely
stepping, in-band fly appeared — slower and humbler than the glued
ones, and real.

**Human:** both catches. This entry exists because the human watched
the videos the agent summarized.
**Agent:** the fixes, and the measures that made flattery harder.
